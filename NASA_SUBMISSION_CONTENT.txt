Copy-ready text:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MONSOON PULSE is an Earth-system trend detection and environmental intelligence platform focused on Kerala, India. Addressing the "Be An Earth System Trend Detective!" challenge, the platform investigates 24 years (2001–2024) of multi-sensor NASA Earth-observation records across all 14 administrative districts to identify what is changing, where change is concentrating, how rapidly shifts are occurring, and whether observed trajectories are statistically defensible under False Discovery Rate control.

We integrated five NASA datasets—GPM IMERG (precipitation), MODIS Terra MOD11A2 (land surface temperature), MODIS MOD13Q1 (vegetation greenness), SMAP SPL4SMGP (root-zone soil moisture), and SRTMGL1 (terrain morphology)—using Google Earth Engine. The platform introduces two compound indicators: HMV (Heat–Moisture–Vegetation) and RTE (Rainfall–Terrain Exposure).

Crucially, MONSOON PULSE reveals statistically significant land-surface warming (+0.24 to +0.36 °C/decade) and localized extreme rainfall intensification across the Western Ghats highlands, providing disaster managers and researchers with an open-source, reproducible screening tool to anticipate compound hydro-meteorological risks.

### 1. What does it do and how does it work?
MONSOON PULSE is an interactive Earth-system trend observatory and screening platform. It systematically investigates environmental changes across all 14 administrative districts of Kerala, India, across a 24-year continuous observation window (2001–2024).

The platform operates through an automated end-to-end scientific pipeline:
1. Satellite Ingestion & Quality Masking: Through Google Earth Engine, we ingest five NASA Earth-observation collections, applying native spatial masking, SRTM-derived land masks to eliminate Arabian Sea marine pixels along Kerala's 590 km coastline, and quality flag filtering (MODIS QC_Day and SummaryQA).
2. Temporal Aggregation: Half-hourly GPM IMERG V07 precipitation rates are summed into calendar-day accumulations (mm/day); 3-hourly SMAP root-zone soil moisture retrievals are composited to daily means; MODIS 8-day LST and 16-day NDVI products are harmonized to annual climatologies.
3. Scientific Indicators:
   - ETCCDI R95p & R95pTOT: Measures annual and South-West monsoon (JJAS) extreme precipitation depth on wet days exceeding the district-specific 95th percentile baseline (2001–2020).
   - HMV (Heat–Moisture–Vegetation): A compound indicator combining standardized Z-scores of daytime LST, root-zone soil moisture, and NDVI over their concurrent multi-sensor window (2015–2024).
   - RTE (Rainfall–Terrain Exposure): A screening indicator scaling standardized extreme monsoon rainfall (Z_R95p,JJAS) by min-max normalized SRTM terrain slope (S_norm).
4. Statistical Trend Rigor: We execute Mann–Kendall monotonic trend tests, assess serial autocorrelation on detrended residuals using the full Yue & Wang (2004) variance correction factor (eta), calculate Theil–Sen median slope rates with mathematically consistent rank-based 95% confidence intervals, and apply Benjamini–Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) control across 70 simultaneous district-variable hypothesis tests (alpha = 0.05).
5. Interactive Delivery: An open-source, zero-login React 19 web platform featuring interactive Leaflet choropleth maps, district exploration profiles, and interactive multi-testing matrix filters.

### 2. What benefits does it have?
- Transparency & Zero Data Fabrication: Unlike black-box machine learning approaches, MONSOON PULSE adheres strictly to deterministic physical formulations. Gaps in satellite coverage are explicitly preserved as NaN; zero interpolation or synthetic data manufacturing is permitted.
- Multi-Sensor Synergy: Moves beyond single-variable climate monitoring by evaluating compound atmospheric, hydrological, vegetative, and geomorphic interactions simultaneously.
- Public Accessibility: Requires no user accounts, API keys, or specialized GIS software to explore 24 years of district-level environmental data.
- Computational Reproducibility: Fully auditable via open-source Python engineering, GEE extraction scripts, and automated unit tests.

### 3. What is the intended impact?
Kerala faces recurring compound climate disasters, including the catastrophic August 2018 Great Flood, the deadly 2019 Kavalappara and Puthumala debris flows, and the tragic July 2024 Chooralmala/Meppadi landslide in Wayanad.

MONSOON PULSE provides regional planners, the Kerala State Disaster Management Authority (KSDMA), local panchayats, and researchers with:
- Objective detection of where extreme precipitation is intensifying and where daytime land heating is accelerating.
- Precursor soil moisture saturation tracking: SMAP root-zone moisture consistently revealed severe saturation (>0.44 m³/m³) prior to regional dam releases and disaster crests.
- Regional exposure screening: Isolates highland districts where steep slope gradients compound with extreme orographic rainfall bursts, informing spatial land-use zoning and disaster preparedness.

### 4. What tools, coding languages, hardware, or software did you use?
- Remote Sensing & Ingestion: Google Earth Engine (Code Editor JavaScript & earthengine-api Python client).
- Scientific Backend & Statistics: Python 3.14, NumPy, Pandas, SciPy (Mann-Kendall, Theil-Sen, Yue-Wang 2004 autocorrelation, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR).
- Quality Assurance & Testing: Pytest (22 automated test suites covering unit conversions, boundary limits, schema conformity, and statistical benchmarks).
- Frontend Architecture: React 19, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Lucide React icons, Recharts (responsive charting), Leaflet / React-Leaflet (choropleth mapping).
- Deployment & CI/CD: GitHub Actions (automated build and testing), GitHub Pages static hosting with SPA routing fallback.
- Open-Source Licensing: Distributed under the permissive Apache License 2.0.

### 5. How is your project creative?
- Project-Defined Compound Indicators: Rather than viewing climate variables in isolation, MONSOON PULSE synthesizes them into actionable indices:
  * HMV = (Z_LST - Z_SMAP - Z_NDVI) / 3 captures concurrent thermal stress, sub-surface hydrological deficit, and ecological strain, diagnosing historical droughts (such as 2016) with high precision.
  * RTE = Z_R95p,JJAS * S_norm integrates atmospheric rainfall extremes with static geomorphology to screen for compound physical slope exposure.
- Dual-Component Deconstruction: In flat deltaic districts like Alappuzha (S_norm = 0.00), the platform explicitly highlights the difference between slope failure hazard (near-zero) and wetland waterlogging exposure (high), displaying rainfall anomalies and slope parameters side-by-side to prevent misinterpretation.
- 30-Second Judge Demonstration Script: Built directly into the user interface, enabling non-technical evaluators to immediately grasp the mission, data integration, statistical rigor, and regional discoveries.

### 6. What factors did your team consider?
- Native Sensor Resolution: Accounted for resolutions ranging from 30 meters (SRTM) to ~10 kilometers (GPM IMERG) using area-weighted spatial polygon reductions.
- Coastal & Lagoon Pixel Contamination: Filtered Arabian Sea marine pixels along Kerala's 590 km coastline to ensure terrestrial accuracy in coastal districts (Alappuzha, Ernakulam, Kollam).
- Mission Observation Windows: Handled the launch timeline of NASA SMAP (April 2015) by restricting the 3-sensor HMV composite to the common 2015–2024 window, avoiding unscientific historical backcasting.
- Independent Ground-Truth Benchmarking: Evaluated satellite rainfall aggregates against published India Meteorological Department (IMD) observational rain-gauge records across Kerala, achieving r = 0.963, R² = 0.927, and Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency NSE = 0.914.
- Ethical Demarcation: Strictly separated remote-sensing exposure screening from site-specific geotechnical landslide prediction, clarifying that RTE does not measure in-situ pore-water pressure or slope stability.

SLIDE 4: SCIENTIFIC METHODOLOGY & STATISTICAL RIGOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Slide Title: Scientific Pipeline & Multi-Testing Discipline
- Methodological Framework:
  * ETCCDI R95p Protocol: Annual & JJAS precipitation on wet days (P >= 1.0mm) exceeding district-specific 95th percentile baseline (2001–2020).
  * Yue & Wang (2004) Modified Mann-Kendall: Trend removed first to prevent conflating physical slopes with autocorrelation; variance inflation factor (eta) applied to Var(S).
  * Theil-Sen Robust Estimator: Median pairwise slopes; rank-based 95% confidence intervals dynamically scaled by corrected variance Var*(S).
  * Benjamini-Hochberg FDR Control: Controls False Discovery Rate at alpha = 0.05 across 70 simultaneous hypothesis tests (14 districts x 5 environmental variables).
- Independent Validation Against Ground-Truth:
  * Benchmarked against India Meteorological Department (IMD) district rain-gauge network: Pearson r = 0.963, R² = 0.927, MAE = 138.4 mm, NSE = 0.914.
- Recommended Visuals: Pipeline architectural flowchart graphic (`diagram_pipeline.png`) and validation metric callout boxes.

SLIDE 5: OUR ANALYTICAL INNOVATION (HMV & RTE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Slide Title: Novel Compound Environmental Indicators
- Indicator 1: HMV (Heat–Moisture–Vegetation Index)
  * Formula: HMV = (Z_LST - Z_SMAP - Z_NDVI) / 3
  * Rationale: Standardized anomalies relative to 2015–2024 multi-sensor window.
  * Physical Meaning: Positive HMV (> +0.5) detects concurrent thermal escalation, soil moisture drydown, and canopy degradation (e.g. 2016 severe drought peak).
- Indicator 2: RTE (Rainfall–Terrain Exposure Screening Indicator)
  * Formula: RTE = Z_R95p,JJAS * S_norm
  * Rationale: Scales standardized monsoon extreme precipitation by min-max normalized terrain slope (S_norm derived from 30m SRTM).
  * Crucial Demarcation: RTE is a macroscopic geographic screening indicator, not a geotechnical site prediction. In flat coastal districts (Alappuzha, S_norm = 0.00), RTE = 0.00 reflects zero slope hazard, while wetland flood risk is captured by raw R95p.
- Recommended Visuals: Dual compound charts (`chart_hmv_rte.png`) showing Palakkad 2016 drought peak and the 2024 district RTE ranking.
